# Spartan Unit Plan

### *Built Like a Tank, Moves Like a Sonic Jet — Lift, Throw, Fight, Run*

## Description

The Spartan Unit Plan is built for one job: produce a soldier's physique that can lift heavy, throw hard, fight in close quarters, and still outrun the man next to him. This isn't a bodybuilding split chasing symmetry for the mirror, and it isn't a pure strength program chasing a bigger number on one lift. It's a hybrid combat-athlete system — dense armor-plate muscle stacked over an engine that never runs out of gas. The end result is an imposing, tank-like frame that still moves with the speed, explosiveness, and recovery capacity of a fighter jet: heavy in the room, fast on the field.

## Core Philosophy & Training Mechanism

- **Total-Body Force Production ("The Tank")**: Squats, Deadlifts, and Presses are trained heavy and often, building the raw structural mass and joint-level strength needed to move, lift, and absorb impact like an armored unit — not just to look strong.
- **Explosive Power Transfer ("The Throw")**: Olympic lift variations (Power Cleans, Push Press) and med-ball/rotational throws train the nervous system to convert raw strength into speed — the same quality needed to launch a spear, a punch, or a sprint start.
- **Combative Capacity ("The Fight")**: Grappling-pattern strength (Zercher Carries, Sled Drags, weighted carries), grip/clinch endurance (Towel Pull-ups, Farmer Carries), and rotational core work (Landmine Twists) build the strength profile of close-quarters combat — controlling, resisting, and overpowering another body.
- **Engine Capacity ("The Jet")**: Sprint intervals, loaded carries for distance, and conditioning circuits are trained as a non-negotiable pillar, not an afterthought — ensuring the mass built stays fast, and the fighter can still perform in the fourth round or the last 200 meters.
- **Durability & Armor (Joint-Prep & Structural Balance)**: Posterior chain work, rotator cuff/scapular stability, and unilateral loading protect the joints from the compounding stress of heavy lifting, striking, and sprinting in the same training week.

## Training Pillars & Exercises

- **Tank (Maximal Strength)**: Back Squat, Deadlift/RDL, Bench Press (Flat/Incline), Zercher Squats, Weighted Pull-ups
- **Throw (Explosive Power)**: Power Cleans, Push Press, Med Ball Rotational Throws, Box Jumps, Kettlebell Swings
- **Fight (Combative Strength & Grip)**: Farmer Carries, Sled Drags/Pushes, Towel Hangs/Pull-ups, Landmine Rotational Core, Battle Ropes
- **Jet (Speed & Conditioning)**: Sprint Intervals (100–400m), Weighted Carry-for-Distance, Assault Bike/Rower Intervals, Agility Ladder Work

## Weekly Training Split (5-Day Hybrid Split)

| Day | Pillar Focus | Key Work |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Tank — Lower Body Strength | Back Squats, Romanian Deadlifts, Walking Lunges, Weighted Carries |
| Day 2 | Throw — Explosive Power & Upper Push | Power Cleans, Push Press, Bench Press, Med Ball Throws |
| Day 3 | Jet — Speed & Conditioning | Sprint Intervals, Sled Pushes, Agility Ladder, Assault Bike Finisher |
| Day 4 | Fight — Grip, Carries & Combative Core | Farmer Carries, Zercher Squats, Towel Pull-ups, Landmine Rotational Core |
| Day 5 | Tank + Jet — Full-Body Density & Engine | Deadlifts, Weighted Pull-ups, Kettlebell Swings, Loaded Carry-for-Distance |

## Nutritional Overview

To perform as a heavy-duty combat unit — lifting near-maximal loads, generating explosive power, grappling under fatigue, and still sprinting at the end of a session — nutrition has to fuel two competing demands at once: the mass and density of a tank, and the recovery speed of a jet engine. Under-fueling here doesn't just slow fat loss, it wrecks explosive output and recovery between sessions. Low-carb approaches are a liability on this plan.

### 1. Caloric Strategy: Performance Surplus

Aim for a moderate surplus that supports both mass gain and high-output conditioning work (+300 to +400 calories above maintenance):

- **Maintenance Estimate**: Multiply your current bodyweight in kg by ~34–36 (or lbs by ~15.5–16.5) given the combined load of heavy lifting, explosive training, and sprint conditioning across the week.
- **Target Intake**: Add 350 kcal to your maintenance baseline. This fuels muscle protein synthesis and glycogen replenishment for back-to-back strength and conditioning sessions without excess fat gain.
- **Reassess every 2–3 weeks**: adjust based on strength progression, sprint times, and bodyweight trend — the goal is mass gain that doesn't cost you speed.

### 2. Macronutrient Target Ranges

| Nutrient | Target Ratio | Purpose in Spartan Unit Training |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | 45% – 50% of calories (~5–6g per kg bodyweight) | Primary fuel for sprint intervals, explosive lifts, and glycogen-hungry combative work; keeps the engine running across a demanding week. |
| Protein | 25% – 30% of calories (~2.0–2.2g per kg bodyweight) | Repairs and builds dense, load-bearing musculature across the posterior chain, grip, and trunk exposed to combined heavy-lift and combat stress. |
| Healthy Fats | 20% – 25% of calories (~0.8–1.0g per kg bodyweight) | Supports testosterone production, joint resilience under heavy axial loading, and sustained energy for longer conditioning efforts. |

### 3. High-Quality Food Selection

**Complex Carbohydrates (Engine Fuel & Glycogen Replenishment)**
- Starchy Tubers: Sweet potatoes, red/white potatoes (fast post-workout glycogen uptake and potassium for muscle contraction).
- Whole Grains: Oats, brown/white basmati rice, quinoa, whole-wheat or sourdough breads.
- Legumes & Pulses: Black beans, lentils, chickpeas (sustained energy plus secondary plant protein).

**High-Yield Proteins (Armor-Plate Muscle & Connective Tissue)**
- Animal Sources: Chicken breast, lean beef, eggs, turkey, fish (salmon, tuna), paneer, low-fat Greek yogurt.
- Plant/Supplement Sources: Whey protein isolate, plant protein blends (pea/rice), tofu, and tempeh.

**Healthy Fats (Hormonal Support & Joint Armor)**
- Whole Sources: Avocados, whole eggs, extra virgin olive oil.
- Nuts & Seeds: Almonds, walnuts, chia seeds, flaxseeds, natural peanut/almond butter.

### 4. Intra-Workout & Timing Strategy

- **Pre-Workout (1.5–2 hrs prior)**: Complex carbs + moderate protein (e.g., oatmeal with protein powder or eggs with whole-grain toast) — ensures full glycogen stores for heavy lifts and sprint work in the same session.
- **Post-Workout (Within 1 hr)**: Fast-digesting carbs + lean protein (e.g., rice with chicken/paneer, or a whey shake with a banana) — drives nutrients into fatigued muscle and replenishes the engine for the next day's output.
- **Daily Hydration & Electrolytes**: 3–4 liters of water plus electrolyte replacement on high-sweat conditioning days — sprint and combative work under fatigue is far more dehydrating than steady-state training.

## Identity Check: What "Tank + Jet" Means in Practice

| Quality | Trained Through | Real-World Output |
|---|---|---|
| Imposing Mass ("Tank") | Heavy squats, deadlifts, presses, weighted carries | Structural strength to lift, carry, and absorb impact without breaking down |
| Explosive Power ("Throw") | Power cleans, push press, med ball throws | Ability to generate and transfer force fast — punches, throws, sprint starts |
| Combative Strength ("Fight") | Farmer carries, sled work, grip/clinch training | Control and overpower an opponent or awkward load under fatigue |
| Sustained Speed ("Jet") | Sprint intervals, conditioning circuits | High output late into a session — the engine doesn't quit when the body is tired |

# Greek God Plan

### *Aesthetic Symmetry — Dense Musculature — Classic Physique Strength*

## Description

The Greek God Training Plan is built for classic bodybuilding aesthetics: balanced proportion, muscle density, and the kind of visual symmetry sculpted into ancient statuary — broad shoulders, a tapered waist, full chest, and sweeping arms. Unlike a functional/tactical split that prioritizes load carriage and grip endurance, or a pure powerlifting split chasing a one-rep max, this plan prioritizes hypertrophy-driven volume, controlled tempo, and targeted isolation work to build size and shape in every visible muscle group, while still training compound lifts heavy enough to keep strength and density climbing alongside aesthetics.

## Core Philosophy & Training Mechanism

- **Muscle Group Isolation for Symmetry**: A classic body-part split (chest, back, shoulders, arms, legs) allows each muscle group to be trained with focused volume and full recovery, sculpting proportion rather than just raw output.
- **Progressive Overload for Density**: Compound lifts (Bench Press, Rows, Squats, Overhead Press) are trained in moderate-to-heavy rep ranges to build the dense, thick muscle bellies associated with a classic physique, not just surface-level pump.
- **Hypertrophy-Optimized Volume**: Multiple angles and rep ranges per muscle group (compound + isolation, 6–15 rep range) maximize time under tension and metabolic stress — the two drivers of muscle growth that a pure strength split under-trains.
- **Mind-Muscle Connection & Tempo Control**: Controlled eccentrics, paused reps, and isolation movements (cable flyes, lateral raises, curls) sharpen the mind-muscle link needed to bring up lagging areas and carve visible separation between muscle groups.
- **V-Taper Engineering**: Deliberate emphasis on lat width (pull-ups, lat pulldowns), shoulder width (lateral raises, overhead press), and a trained-down waist creates the broad-shoulders-to-narrow-waist silhouette that defines the aesthetic look.

## Training Exercises

- Bench Press (Flat/Incline) + Cable/Dumbbell Flyes
- Back Squats + Leg Press + Walking Lunges
- Deadlifts (Conventional/RDL) for posterior chain thickness
- Overhead Press + Lateral Raises + Rear Delt Flyes
- Weighted Pull-ups / Lat Pulldowns + Barbell/Cable Rows
- Barbell/Dumbbell Curls + Skull Crushers / Cable Pushdowns
- Weighted Dips + Close-Grip Bench Press
- Calf Raises + Core Circuit (hanging leg raises, cable crunches)

## Weekly Training Split (5-Day Body-Part Split)

| Day | Focus | Key Lifts |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Chest & Triceps | Flat Bench Press, Incline DB Press, Cable Flyes, Weighted Dips, Skull Crushers |
| Day 2 | Back & Rear Delts | Weighted Pull-ups, Barbell Rows, Lat Pulldowns, Seated Cable Rows, Rear Delt Flyes |
| Day 3 | Legs | Back Squats, Romanian Deadlifts, Leg Press, Walking Lunges, Calf Raises |
| Day 4 | Shoulders & Arms | Overhead Press, Lateral Raises, Barbell Curls, Cable Pushdowns, Hammer Curls |
| Day 5 | Full-Body Density + Core | Deadlifts, Incline Bench, Weighted Pull-ups, Hanging Leg Raises, Cable Crunches |

## Nutritional Overview

To build a dense, symmetrical physique — the Greek God look — nutrition needs to prioritize a consistent protein surplus and enough overall calories to fuel hypertrophy across every session. This is a size-and-shape goal, not a pure performance or fat-loss goal, so the approach leans harder into protein and total volume of food than the Spartan plan's functional-fuel approach.

### 1. Caloric Strategy: Lean Bulk (Moderate Surplus)

To maximize muscle growth while keeping fat gain controlled, aim for a moderate caloric surplus of +300 to +500 calories above maintenance:

- **Maintenance Estimate**: Multiply your current bodyweight in kg by ~33–35 (or lbs by ~15–16) for a moderately active lifestyle with 5 weekly training sessions.
- **Target Intake**: Add 350–400 kcal to your maintenance baseline. This surplus is large enough to drive consistent muscle protein synthesis (MPS) and glycogen replenishment without excessive fat gain.
- **Reassess every 2–3 weeks**: if bodyweight gain exceeds ~0.5% per week, trim 100–150 kcal to keep the bulk lean.

### 2. Macronutrient Target Ranges

| Nutrient | Target Ratio | Purpose in Greek God Training |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | 30% – 35% of calories (~2.0–2.4g per kg bodyweight) | Maximizes muscle protein synthesis for dense, full muscle bellies across chest, back, shoulders, and arms. |
| Carbohydrates | 40% – 45% of calories (~4–5g per kg bodyweight) | Fuels high-volume hypertrophy training and replenishes glycogen for the next session's pump and performance. |
| Healthy Fats | 20% – 25% of calories (~0.8–1.0g per kg bodyweight) | Supports testosterone production, joint health under heavier compound loading, and satiety across a surplus diet. |

### 3. High-Quality Food Selection

**Complex Carbohydrates (Glycogen & Training Volume)**
- Starchy Staples: White/brown rice, potatoes, sweet potatoes, oats.
- Whole Grains: Quinoa, whole-wheat pasta, sourdough, whole-grain bread.
- Fruit for Micronutrients & Insulin Response: Bananas, berries, mangoes (great pre/post workout).

**High-Yield Proteins (Density & Recovery)**
- Animal Sources: Chicken breast/thigh, lean beef, eggs and egg whites, turkey, fish (salmon, tuna, tilapia), paneer, cottage cheese, Greek yogurt.
- Plant/Supplement Sources: Whey protein isolate/concentrate, casein (before bed), tofu, tempeh, plant protein blends.

**Healthy Fats (Hormonal Support)**
- Whole Sources: Whole eggs, avocado, extra virgin olive oil, ghee (in moderation).
- Nuts & Seeds: Almonds, walnuts, peanut butter, chia and flax seeds.

### 4. Meal & Nutrient Timing Strategy

- **Pre-Workout (1.5–2 hrs prior)**: Complex carbs + moderate protein + low fat (e.g., rice with chicken, or oats with whey) to fuel high-volume training without sluggishness.
- **Intra/Post-Workout (Within 45 min)**: Fast carbs + fast protein (e.g., whey shake with a banana, or white rice with egg whites) to spike insulin and shuttle nutrients into fatigued muscle for repair and growth.
- **Before Bed**: Slow-digesting protein (casein, cottage cheese, or Greek yogurt) to sustain amino acid availability through the overnight anabolic window.
- **Daily Hydration**: 3–4 liters of water to support nutrient transport, joint health under higher training volume, and optimal muscle fullness/pump.

## Summary: Spartan vs. Greek God — Training Intent

| Attribute | Spartan Plan | Greek God Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Goal | Functional strength, load carriage, grip endurance | Aesthetic symmetry, muscle size, and density |
| Training Style | Compound, multi-planar, unstable-load focus | Body-part split, hypertrophy volume, isolation-focused |
| Rep Ranges | Low-to-moderate, endurance-under-load emphasis | Moderate-to-high (6–15), time-under-tension emphasis |
| Caloric Strategy | Slight surplus / maintenance (+200–300 kcal) | Moderate lean-bulk surplus (+300–500 kcal) |
| Protein Target | 1.8–2.2 g/kg | 2.0–2.4 g/kg |
| Signature Movements | Farmer Carries, Zercher Squats, Towel Hangs | Incline Bench, Lat Pulldowns, Lateral Raises |

# Superhero Plan

### *High Performance, Imposing Physique, Bruce Wayne-Level Readiness*

## Description

The Superhero Plan is built for a very specific archetype: the billionaire vigilante, not the powerlifter and not the bodybuilder. Think Bruce Wayne — a physique that looks commanding in a tailored suit and moves like a weapon in a fight. This plan sits deliberately between three worlds: the size and shape of aesthetic training, the raw output of strength training, and the work capacity of an endurance/power athlete. Nothing is trained to one extreme. The result is a lean, dense, capable frame built on weighted calisthenics and barbell fundamentals — imposing at rest, dangerous in motion.

## Core Philosophy & Training Mechanism

- **Weighted Calisthenics as the Backbone**: High-volume weighted pull-ups, dips, and push-up variations build relative strength — strength-to-bodyweight — producing the lean, athletic density of someone who can move their own mass explosively, not just move a bar.
- **Iron Barbell Core Movements**: Squats, Deadlifts, Overhead Press, and Bench Press remain the structural foundation — trained in a push/pull/legs/core split to build raw strength and joint-level resilience under the calisthenics volume.
- **V-Taper & Structural Symmetry**: Deliberate emphasis on lat width (weighted pull-ups, wide-grip work), shoulder capping (lateral raises, handstand push-ups), and a trained-down waist/core builds the broad-shoulders-to-narrow-waist silhouette — imposing by proportion, not just mass.
- **Hybrid Conditioning (Power + Endurance)**: Sprint work, rowing/assault bike intervals, and circuit-style conditioning are trained alongside strength work so output doesn't collapse under fatigue — the plan assumes you may need to fight, chase, or escape after already being tired.
- **Functional Toughness Over Isolation**: Grip strength, rotational core stability, and unilateral loading (single-arm/single-leg variations) are trained deliberately, building the kind of full-body control and durability that translates directly to real combative or athletic scenarios, not just the mirror.

## Training Split & Exercises (Push / Pull / Legs / Core)

- **Push (Chest, Shoulders, Triceps)**: Weighted Dips, Incline Bench Press, Handstand Push-ups (or Pike Push-ups), Overhead Press, Lateral Raises
- **Pull (Back, Lats, Traps, Biceps)**: Weighted Pull-ups (wide + neutral grip), Barbell Rows, Weighted Chin-ups, Face Pulls, Barbell Curls
- **Legs (Strength + Power)**: Back Squats, Romanian Deadlifts, Bulgarian Split Squats, Box Jumps, Weighted Lunges
- **Core & Conditioning (Stability + Engine)**: Hanging Leg Raises, Weighted Planks, Landmine Rotational Twists, Sprint Intervals, Rowing/Assault Bike Finishers

## Weekly Training Split (5-Day Push/Pull/Legs + Hybrid Conditioning)

| Day | Focus | Key Work |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Push — Chest, Shoulders, Triceps | Weighted Dips, Incline Bench Press, Handstand Push-ups, Lateral Raises |
| Day 2 | Pull — Back, Lats, Traps, Biceps | Weighted Pull-ups, Barbell Rows, Face Pulls, Barbell Curls |
| Day 3 | Legs — Strength & Power | Back Squats, Romanian Deadlifts, Bulgarian Split Squats, Box Jumps |
| Day 4 | Core & Conditioning — Engine Work | Hanging Leg Raises, Weighted Planks, Landmine Twists, Sprint Intervals |
| Day 5 | Full-Body Hybrid — Density + Power | Weighted Chin-ups, Overhead Press, Weighted Lunges, Assault Bike Finisher |

## Nutritional Overview

To build a lean, imposing, high-performance frame — dense enough to look commanding, lean enough to move fast, and fueled enough to sustain both strength and conditioning work — nutrition has to blend a bodybuilder's protein focus with a performance athlete's carbohydrate demand. This is a recomposition-and-performance goal at once: visible muscle and low body fat, without sacrificing output.

### 1. Caloric Strategy: Lean Performance Maintenance

Aim for tight eucaloric maintenance, or a very slight surplus, to build lean tissue while keeping body fat low enough for the aesthetic to show (+100 to +250 calories above maintenance):

- **Maintenance Estimate**: Multiply your current bodyweight in kg by ~32–34 (or lbs by ~14.5–15.5) given the combined load of high-volume calisthenics, barbell strength work, and conditioning across the week.
- **Target Intake**: Add 150–250 kcal to your maintenance baseline on training days; drop to maintenance or a slight deficit on rest days to keep visible leanness intact.
- **Reassess every 2–3 weeks**: track waist measurement and strength/conditioning benchmarks together — the goal is size and definition rising in parallel, not one at the cost of the other.

### 2. Macronutrient Target Ranges

| Nutrient | Target Ratio | Purpose in Superhero Training |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | 30% – 35% of calories (~2.0–2.3g per kg bodyweight) | Maintains and builds dense, visible musculature under a tight caloric window — the bodybuilding half of the equation. |
| Carbohydrates | 35% – 40% of calories (~3.5–4.5g per kg bodyweight) | Fuels high-volume weighted calisthenics, sprint work, and conditioning finishers — the performance-athlete half of the equation. |
| Healthy Fats | 25% – 30% of calories (~0.9–1.1g per kg bodyweight) | Supports hormonal health and satiety on a leaner caloric intake, plus joint resilience under high training volume. |

### 3. High-Quality Food Selection

**Complex Carbohydrates (Training Fuel, Kept Lean)**
- Starchy Staples: Sweet potatoes, white/brown rice, oats — timed around training rather than spread flat through the day.
- Whole Grains & Fruit: Quinoa, whole-wheat bread, berries, bananas — for micronutrients and fast pre/post-workout fuel.

**High-Yield Lean Proteins (Density Without Excess Fat)**
- Animal Sources: Chicken breast, lean beef, egg whites + whole eggs, turkey, white fish, tuna, low-fat Greek yogurt, cottage cheese.
- Plant/Supplement Sources: Whey protein isolate, tofu, tempeh, plant protein blends.

**Healthy Fats (Hormonal Support, Controlled Portions)**
- Whole Sources: Avocado, extra virgin olive oil, whole eggs (in moderation given lean-protein emphasis).
- Nuts & Seeds: Almonds, walnuts, chia and flax seeds — measured portions to avoid tipping caloric balance.

### 4. Meal & Nutrient Timing Strategy

- **Pre-Workout (1.5–2 hrs prior)**: Moderate carbs + lean protein (e.g., rice with chicken, or oats with egg whites) — enough fuel for high-volume calisthenics without feeling weighed down.
- **Post-Workout (Within 45 min)**: Fast carbs + fast protein (e.g., whey shake with a banana, or white rice with lean beef) — recovery for both strength and conditioning demands.
- **Evening**: Slow protein + fibrous vegetables (e.g., cottage cheese with greens) — supports overnight repair while keeping calories lean.
- **Daily Hydration**: 3–4 liters of water, with electrolytes on high-conditioning days — sprint and circuit work under a leaner diet dehydrates faster than steady bodybuilding-style training.

# Manga / Anime Plan

### *The Aesthetic-Functional Physique — Toji, Baki, Sun Ken Rock Archetype*

## Description

The Manga/Anime Plan is built around the physique archetype that shows up again and again in manga and anime combat leads: not a bulky powerlifter, not a stage bodybuilder, but a lean, densely-muscled, highly-capable frame — broad back, sharp arm and chest shape, a visibly trained core, and the kind of raw bodyweight mastery that reads as "trained for years, not for a photoshoot." Think Toji Fushiguro, Baki Hanma, or Sun Ken Rock. This is the one plan in the collection built around a real, structured calisthenics skill progression (muscle-ups, levers, one-arm work) layered with just enough bodybuilding-style isolation to sharpen arm, chest, and shoulder shape.

## Core Philosophy & Training Mechanism

- **Weighted & High-Volume Calisthenics as the Foundation**: Pull-ups, dips, and push-up variations trained for high volume and added load build the lean, dense, relative-strength physique that defines the archetype — strength that looks earned, not just heavy.
- **Bodybuilding-Style Shape Work (Lats, Arms, Chest)**: Targeted isolation — cable flyes, curls, pushdowns, lat pulldown variations — is layered on top of the calisthenics base specifically to sharpen visible shape and separation, the detail work a pure calisthenics routine tends to skip.
- **Leg Isolation Over Heavy Compound Bias**: Leg press, cable leg extensions, and leg curls are prioritized over max-effort squats/deadlifts — building visible quad sweep and hamstring shape without adding the bulk or joint fatigue that would work against the lean, agile silhouette.
- **Delt Width & Postural Armor**: Side lateral raises build the capped, wide-shoulder look core to the archetype, while face pulls build rear-delt and upper-back health to support the heavy pulling volume and keep posture upright under high training loads.
- **Structured Calisthenics Skill Progression**: This is the only plan in the set built around a real progression toward advanced bodyweight skills — muscle-ups, one-arm push-up and chin-up progressions, L-sits, and front/back levers — trained deliberately as skill work, not just as accessory movements.
- **Advanced Core Training**: Levers, L-sits, and hanging progressions demand a level of core control far beyond standard crunches or planks, building the visibly trained, functional midsection seen across the reference archetypes.

## Training Split & Exercises

- **Push (Chest, Shoulders, Triceps + Shape Work)**: Weighted Dips, Incline Push-ups/Bench Press, Cable Flyes, Side Lateral Raises, Cable Pushdowns
- **Pull (Back, Lats, Traps + Arm Shape)**: Weighted Pull-ups, Lat Pulldowns (wide grip), Face Pulls, Barbell/Cable Curls, Rows
- **Legs (Isolation-Biased)**: Leg Press, Cable Leg Extensions, Leg Curls, Calf Raises, Walking Lunges (light/moderate load)
- **Calisthenics Skill Work (Dedicated Sessions)**: Muscle-up Progressions, One-Arm Push-up Progressions, One-Arm Chin-up Progressions, L-Sit Holds, Front/Back Lever Progressions
- **Advanced Core**: Hanging L-Sits, Dragon Flags, Front Lever Tuck Holds, Hollow Body Holds, Weighted Hanging Leg Raises

|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Push + Shape Work | Weighted Dips, Incline Bench/Push-ups, Cable Flyes, Side Lateral Raises |
| Day 2 | Pull + Arm Shape | Weighted Pull-ups, Lat Pulldowns, Face Pulls, Cable Curls |
| Day 3 | Calisthenics Skill Work | Muscle-up Progressions, One-Arm Push-up Drills, L-Sit Holds, Lever Progressions |

## Nutritional Overview

# Athlete Plan

### *The Functional Athlete — Virat Kohli / Cristiano Ronaldo Archetype*

## Description

The Athlete Plan is built around the functional-athlete archetype rather than a gym-first aesthetic: a Virat Kohli or Cristiano Ronaldo-style physique, where the training exists to serve the sport, and the physique is simply the visible byproduct of years of skill work, strength work, and power development done in balance. Nothing here is trained to look a certain way in isolation — the leanness, muscle tone, and athletic V-shape all emerge from a training approach that treats skill training and physical training as equally non-negotiable, never one at the expense of the other.

## Core Philosophy & Training Mechanism

- **Skill Training as a Co-Equal Pillar**: Sport-specific skill work (batting/footwork drills, ball-striking, agility patterns) is programmed with the same priority as strength work, not treated as separate from "real" training — this is what separates a functional athlete's physique from a gym-built one.
- **Strength as the Structural Base**: Squats, deadlifts, presses, and rows are trained in moderate volume year-round to build the durable strength foundation that supports both power output and injury resilience across a long competitive season.
- **Power Development (Force Transfer)**: Olympic lift variations, plyometrics, and med-ball throws convert raw strength into the explosive rotational and linear power needed for a cover drive, a sprint past a defender, or a change of direction.
- **Functional Movement Over Bodybuilding Isolation**: Unilateral work (split squats, single-leg RDLs), rotational core training, and multi-planar movement dominate over isolation exercises — the goal is transferable athletic capacity, not muscle-by-muscle sculpting.
- **Balanced Conditioning (Aerobic Base + Repeat-Sprint Capacity)**: A mix of aerobic base work and repeat-sprint/interval training builds the engine needed to maintain skill execution and power output deep into a match or innings, not just in the first twenty minutes.

## Training Pillars & Exercises

- **Strength (Structural Base)**: Back Squat, Deadlift/RDL, Bench Press, Barbell Rows, Overhead Press
- **Power (Force Transfer)**: Power Cleans, Box Jumps, Med Ball Rotational Throws, Depth Jumps, Kettlebell Swings
- **Functional/Unilateral (Injury Resilience)**: Bulgarian Split Squats, Single-Leg RDLs, Lateral Lunges, Copenhagen Planks, Rotational Cable Chops
- **Skill Training (Sport-Specific)**: Batting/Footwork Drills or Ball-Striking Technical Work, Reaction and Agility Ladder Drills, Small-Sided Games or Match Simulation
- **Conditioning (Aerobic Base + Repeat-Sprint)**: Zone 2 Running or Cycling, Repeat-Sprint Intervals (10–30m), Shuttle Runs

## Weekly Training Split (Skill + Strength Balanced, 6-Day Cycle)

| Day | Focus | Key Work |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Strength (Lower) + Skill AM/PM | Back Squats, Single-Leg RDLs, Copenhagen Planks + Skill Session |
| Day 2 | Power + Skill AM/PM | Power Cleans, Box Jumps, Med Ball Throws + Skill Session |
| Day 3 | Conditioning + Light Skill Work | Zone 2 Base Run, Repeat-Sprint Intervals, Technical Drilling |

| Day 6 | Match Simulation / Small-Sided Games | Full Skill Expression Under Fatigue — Game-Speed Conditions |

## Nutritional Overview

To perform as a functional athlete — executing fine motor skill under fatigue, generating repeated power output, and recovering fast enough to train or compete again the next day — nutrition needs to prioritize both high carbohydrate availability and high protein intake, with fat kept moderate to avoid displacing either. This is a performance-first approach: physique benefits follow from consistent, well-fueled training, not from deliberately restricting intake.

### 1. Caloric Strategy: Performance-Matched Intake

Aim for intake matched closely to daily output, adjusting upward on double-session or match days (maintenance to +300 calories depending on training/competition load):

- **Maintenance Estimate**: Multiply your current bodyweight in kg by ~35–38 (or lbs by ~16–17.5) given the combined load of skill sessions, strength work, and conditioning — higher than a pure aesthetic plan due to daily two-a-day training volume.
- **Target Intake**: Stay near maintenance on lighter skill/technical days; add 200–300 kcal on double-session strength + skill days or match days to match the higher energy demand.
- **Reassess weekly**: performance athletes should track energy levels, skill execution quality, and recovery markers alongside bodyweight — under-fueling shows up as skill breakdown before it shows up on the scale.

### 2. Macronutrient Target Ranges

| Nutrient | Target Ratio | Purpose in Athlete Training |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | 45% – 55% of calories (~5–7g per kg bodyweight) | Primary fuel for skill sessions, repeat-sprint conditioning, and match-day output; the highest carb emphasis across the plan set given daily two-a-day demands. |
| Protein | 25% – 30% of calories (~1.8–2.2g per kg bodyweight) | Supports recovery and lean tissue maintenance across a high-frequency training week without displacing the carbohydrate needed for performance. |
| Healthy Fats | 18% – 22% of calories (~0.7–0.9g per kg bodyweight) | Kept moderate and deliberately controlled so it doesn't crowd out carbohydrate or protein needs on high-volume days. |

### 3. High-Quality Food Selection

**Complex Carbohydrates (The Primary Fuel Source)**
- Starchy Staples: White/brown rice, potatoes, sweet potatoes, pasta — sized generously around training and match days.
- Whole Grains & Fruit: Oats, whole-wheat bread, bananas, dates, berries — for both sustained and fast-release energy.
- Legumes: Lentils, chickpeas, black beans — secondary carb/protein source for recovery meals.

**High-Yield Proteins (Recovery-Focused)**
- Animal Sources: Chicken breast, lean beef, eggs, fish (salmon, tuna), turkey, low-fat dairy (paneer, Greek yogurt).
- Plant/Supplement Sources: Whey protein isolate, plant protein blends, tofu, tempeh.

**Healthy Fats (Controlled, Not Restricted)**
- Whole Sources: Avocado, olive oil, whole eggs — moderate portions to preserve room for carbohydrate.
- Nuts & Seeds: Almonds, walnuts, peanut butter — useful for calorie-dense recovery snacks between sessions.

### 4. Meal & Nutrient Timing Strategy

- **Pre-Session (2–3 hrs prior)**: High complex carbs + moderate protein (e.g., rice with chicken, or oats with fruit) — full glycogen stores for skill execution and repeated power output.
- **During Long Sessions/Matches**: Fast carbs (sports drink, banana, dates) to sustain blood glucose and delay fatigue-driven skill breakdown.
- **Post-Session (Within 30–45 min)**: Fast carbs + fast protein (e.g., whey shake with a banana, or rice with fish) — rapid glycogen and protein resynthesis before the next session, often the same day.
- **Daily Hydration & Electrolytes**: 3.5–4.5 liters of water plus electrolyte replacement on high-sweat training or match days — hydration status directly affects reaction time and skill execution.
